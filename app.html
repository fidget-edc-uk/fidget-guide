    Premium fidgets, explained simply.

    Premium fidgets are precision-made objects you play with in one hand, usually machined from metal and held together by magnets, springs or a bearing. Three choices decide almost everything about how one feels.

    Eight families of fidget. Tap any card to go deeper. Tuning forks, a ringing feature found on several of them, have their own page.

    Mechanisms

    How they work

    Four mechanisms create most of the feedback, with friction tracks and tuning forks adding two more.

    Magnets+
    They pull parts into set positions for a snap or "homing" feel. Stronger magnets mean a firmer snap. More on magnets →
    Mechanical clicks+
    Spring-loaded balls or pins drop into notches, much like the click of a pen. More on springs and clicks →
    Bearings+
    They let spinners and rings turn smoothly. Stainless and hybrid ceramic bearings are both common; full ceramic is an upgrade for a longer spin. More on bearings →
    Friction+
    A Teflon (PTFE) block clamps and slides along a track, giving smooth, even, damped travel instead of hard stops. Friction sliders →
    Tuning forks+
    A metal fork that rings with a "ding" when something strikes it, found on sliders, floating sliders and spinners. Tuning forks →
    Springs+
    They return buttons and levers for a push-and-release click. More on springs →

    Collectors generally agree that a well-made fidget feels clean and identical every time, and that this precision is what separates premium pieces from cheap copies.

    Materials

    What it's made of.

    The material changes how a fidget feels, sounds and ages. This covers the toy itself, then the parts inside it and the accessories around it.

    Body materialsPlates and insertsFinishesChoosingButtonsBeadsBallsBead materialsSpringsAccessories
    The toy itself

    Body materials

    The mechanism stays the same; the material changes how heavy it feels, how it sounds and how it ages. Tap any material to see what it's made of and how it behaves, or compare two side by side.

    Heavier = more momentum

    Heavier metals spin longer and land with a deeper, more solid impact; the snap itself comes from the magnets. Lighter ones suit all-day pocket carry.

    Heavier bodies ring lower

    Heavier bodies, and metals sound travels through more slowly, such as zirconium, ring lower and chunkier. Lighter titanium sounds brighter. Shape and what strikes what matter too.

    Mixed builds are common

    Many pieces pair materials, such as a titanium body with a copper plate or a steel body with brass.

    The toy itself

    Plates, tracks and inserts

    The parts inside a slider or spinner are often a different material from the body, chosen for how they move or sound.

    PTFE (Teflon)

    Very low friction. Used for slide blocks, films and plates for smooth travel, and sold as spare stickers. Grit caught between plates scratches it easily.

    PEEK

    A tough engineering plastic used for tracks and impact plates, such as the impact plate in the Lautie Choc.

    PEI (Ultem)

    An amber, see-through plastic, among the lightest materials used, for bodies and plates.

    Sapphire windows

    Clear, scratch-resistant windows offered on some pieces so you can see inside.

    Accent plates

    Decorative plates in contrasting or patterned metals; some are held by magnets and swap out.

    Finishes

    The surface on top

    The finish mostly changes grip and how well marks hide.

    Stonewashed

    A matte, tumbled look that hides scratches best.

    Blasted

    An even, flat matte with good grip.

    Satin

    Fine brushed lines with a soft sheen: cleaner than stonewashed, less shiny than polished.

    Polished

    Shiny; looks great but shows every mark and fingerprint.

    Coloured

    Anodised or heat-coloured titanium, or heat-treated black zirconium, in blues, purples, golds, black or "oil slick".

    DLC

    A thin, hard black coating that resists scratches; it can still wear through on edges over years.

    Patina

    Copper and brass darken naturally with use.

    Weight isn't everything: the magnets, plates and how the toy is built change the feel and sound as much as the metal does. On spinners, a denser rim keeps them going for longer.

    Parts

    Buttons and caps

    On spinners and rings, the buttons are the parts you pinch or hold.

    They don't spin

    Buttons stay still in your grip while the body turns, so their material changes look, weight in the hand and grip more than spin time.

    Matching or contrasting

    Often the same metal as the body, or a contrast such as brass, copper or zirconium.

    Exotic buttons

    Patterned metals such as Mokuti are a popular upgrade on high-end spinners.

    Shape

    The shape and size of the button change how it sits between your fingers.

    Parts

    Beads

    Beads turn up in beaded spinners, on lanyards and as pendants.

    Beaded spinners

    Beads installed in the space round the bearing or in the body. Options can include stainless steel, ceramic, titanium and ruby.

    Spacers

    Small plastic or metal spacers sit between beads on some designs.

    Lanyard beads and pendants

    Decorative beads threaded on a lanyard or worn as a pendant; a category of their own at many shops.

    Rule of thumb

    Harder, heavier beads tend to sound sharper and louder; lighter ones are softer and quieter.

    Parts

    Balls

    Small balls do three different jobs: they make clicks, strike forks and carry bearings.

    Click balls

    In mechanical sliders, a spring pushes a ball or bead into notches. Zirconia ceramic is common, and size matters: one slider takes 3 mm and 3.175 mm beads for different feels.

    Strikers

    In tuning-fork pieces, balls or beads hit the fork. Hard zirconia feels bouncy; POM plastic gives a clearer ring. Steel balls are used in some fork spinners.

    Bearing balls

    Steel balls in standard bearings, ceramic balls in hybrid and full ceramic bearings.

    Parts

    Springs

    The spring under a click bead sets how firm the click is.

    Steel or bronze

    Steel springs are the norm. Phosphor-bronze springs are about half as stiff for the same size and ring lower. Gold-plated stainless springs are common in spare kits.

    Size sets stiffness

    Stiffness rises very steeply with wire thickness, and more coils make a spring softer. Plating or colour makes no difference.

    Wear

    Springs soften with use; a fresh one brings a mushy click back.

    Accessories

    Accessories

    The extras around a fidget, for carrying it, protecting it and tuning it.

    Lanyards and paracord

    Many pieces have a lanyard hole for a cord or loop.

    Pouches and cases

    Protect pieces from scratches and pocket grit.

    Tuning kits

    Washers, spare magnets, springs and beads for changing the feel.

    Spare parts

    Teflon stickers, rubber pads, plates and bearings.

    Tools

    Magnet pullers and holders for lifting magnets out when tuning.
